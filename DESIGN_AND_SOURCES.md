# Design, Visuals and Sources

## 1. PowerPoint master specification

- **Canvas:** 16:9 widescreen
- **Background:** white or very light neutral gray
- **Primary color:** deep navy, approximately `#12304A`
- **Accent:** Slovenian red, approximately `#C8102E`; use sparingly
- **Secondary accent:** muted blue, approximately `#5B8DB8`
- **Title font:** Aptos Display, Arial, or Helvetica, 30–36 pt
- **Body font:** Aptos, Arial, or Helvetica, 20–24 pt
- **Footer:** 10–12 pt, slide number, short source label, presenter affiliation
- **Rule:** one main idea per slide; no more than six bullets where possible

Use English as the visual language for the main deck. For a bilingual room, put the Chinese translation in a lower panel with a thin divider, or keep the Chinese in the notes and distribute a bilingual handout. Avoid placing a full English paragraph and a full Chinese paragraph on every slide because readability will suffer.

## 3. Chart suggestions

Do not add numerical charts unless current data have been confirmed. Appropriate non-numerical diagrams include:

- Research portfolio wheel
- Fundamental-to-application pipeline
- Materials-to-device battery chain
- Chemistry–biology interface
- Circular economy loop
- Atomic-to-device characterization pyramid
- University–NIC–industry Venn diagram
- Six-step collaboration roadmap

If current statistics are obtained from NIC, useful charts could show research personnel, doctoral researchers, publications, patents, international projects, funding sources, or industry collaboration. Every number should include a year and a source on the slide.

## 4. Source register

### Primary institutional source

- National Institute of Chemistry, “About the Institute”: https://www.ki.si/en/about-the-institute/

Use this source for the institute’s official mission, structure, research areas, current leadership, current facilities and official contact information. Check the page immediately before finalizing the presentation.

### European technology-centre profile

- European Commission, Monitor Industrial Ecosystems, “National Institute of Chemistry Slovenia / Kemijski Inštitut”: https://monitor-industrial-ecosystems.ec.europa.eu/technology-centre/national-institute-chemistry-slovenia-kemijski-institut

Use this as a secondary profile for infrastructure and technology-centre context. Confirm all equipment descriptions and historical claims against current NIC pages.

### Institute archive / historical context

- National Institute of Chemistry, archived English “About the Institute” page: https://arhiv.ki.si/en/about-the-institute/

Use only for historical or background context, and label archived information clearly.

### National and European context

- European Union research and innovation portal: https://research-and-innovation.ec.europa.eu/
- European Research Area: https://research-and-innovation.ec.europa.eu/strategy/strategy-research-and-innovation/european-research-area_en

Use these for general statements about European research cooperation; do not present them as NIC-specific evidence.

## 5. Slide citation policy

- Add a small footer such as `Source: NIC, About the Institute (accessed [date])` on institutional slides.
- Add the full URL to the final reference slide or handout.
- Cite the actual image creator or licence next to every externally sourced image.
- Do not state that a facility is open to external users until NIC confirms the access procedure.
- Do not state current staff numbers, budgets, funding percentages, patent rankings or project totals without a dated official source.
- Treat third-party summaries as orientation material, not as the final authority.

## 7. Final editorial checklist

- Confirm the official English name and preferred abbreviation.
- Confirm whether “National Institute of Chemistry” or “National Chemical Institute” is preferred in the conference language.
- Confirm the current list of departments and centres.
- Confirm all equipment names, models and external-access claims.
- Confirm any China–Slovenia cooperation examples.
- Add the presenter’s email, institute, and a collaboration contact at NIC.
- Rehearse to 40–42 minutes, leaving 3–5 minutes for questions.
